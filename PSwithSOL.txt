1. PROBLEM STATEMENT, DESCRIPTION, OR BACKGROUND
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 OFFICIAL BACKGROUND
The National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre (NCIIPC) 
assesses the cyber resilience of Critical Sector Entities (CSEs).

As part of these assessments, NCIIPC performs manual reviews of samples of 
security alerts and case-management records generated by Security Operations 
Centres (SOCs). These reviews have consistently produced valuable supervisory 
findings that were not evident through policies, audits, self-assessments, 
management reports, KPI dashboards, or compliance documentation.

The purpose of these reviews is not to assess individual alerts. Rather, alert 
and case-management data are used as operational evidence to assess whether a 
CSE possesses effective capabilities relating to:
  (i)   Threat Detection
  (ii)  Investigation
  (iii) Escalation
  (iv)  Incident Response
  (v)   Security Operations
  (vi)  Governance and Oversight
  (vii) Operational Discipline
  (viii)Cyber Resilience

While effective, manual review is resource-intensive and difficult to scale 
across a growing number of CSEs and increasing volumes of security data.


1.2 DESCRIPTION
NCIIPC seeks a deployable Supervisory Analytics Tool for SOC Assessment 
(SAT-SA) that assists supervisors in analysing SOC alert and case-management 
data at scale. The tool should help supervisors:
  (i)   Identify entities requiring supervisory attention.
  (ii)  Prioritise alert samples and investigations for manual review.
  (iii) Detect operational weaknesses and cyber resilience concerns.
  (iv)  Improve the efficiency, consistency and scalability of supervisory 
        assessments.

The tool is intended to support human examiners and supervisory decision-making. 
It is not intended to replace supervisory judgement.


1.3 OUT OF SCOPE
The proposed solution is not intended to:
  (i)   Function or replace as a Security Operations Centre (SOC) of CSEs.
  (ii)  Perform real-time monitoring.
  (iii) Act as a SIEM platform.
  (iv)  Act as a centralized SOC for multiple entities.
  (v)   Continuously collect logs or telemetry from CSEs.
  (vi)  Serve as a national cyber monitoring platform.

The solution should be viewed as a supervisory analytics capability, not an 
operational security capability.


1.4 DATA ENVIRONMENT
Participants may assume access to periodic submissions including:
  (i)   Alert metadata
  (ii)  Case management records
  (iii) Investigation workflow data
  (iv)  Escalation records
  (v)   Alert disposition and closure information
  (vi)  Asset and system inventory information (where available)

Solutions should minimise dependence on raw logs, packet captures, customer 
information, or other sensitive operational data unless clearly justified.


1.5 CORE SUPERVISORY PROBLEM
Manual reviews often uncover weaknesses that are not visible through 
conventional reporting mechanisms. These weaknesses generally fall into two 
categories:

A. Execution Gaps:
Situations where documented controls, governance arrangements, policies, 
procedures, metrics or reported capabilities suggest effective operation, 
but operational evidence indicates otherwise. Examples include:
  (i)   Alerts acknowledged but not meaningfully investigated.
  (ii)  Cases closed unusually quickly.
  (iii) Critical alerts closed without escalation.
  (iv)  Repetitive or template-driven investigations.
  (v)   Controls deployed but not effectively monitored.
  (vi)  Operational behaviour designed to satisfy metrics without reducing risk.

B. Negative Space:
Situations where expected evidence is absent. Examples include:
  (i)   Missing telemetry from critical systems.
  (ii)  Absence of expected alert categories.
  (iii) Missing investigations or escalation records.
  (iv)  Unexpectedly low activity levels.
  (v)   Monitoring blind spots.
  (vi)  Absence of evidence that would normally be expected within comparable 
        environments.

The tool should help supervisors identify both known and previously unknown 
indicators of these conditions.


1.6 KEY FUNCTIONAL AND DEPLOYMENT REQUIREMENTS
- Data Ingestion: Ingest structured multi-entity data (CSV, JSON, DB dumps) 
  across historical assessment periods.
- Supervisory Analytics: Detect execution gaps, negative space, anomalies, 
  cohort deviations, and generate entity-level risk prioritizations.
- Explainability & Traceability: Provide clear rationale, exact evidence 
  pointers, and full auditability for every flagged finding.
- Strict Air-Gap Mandate: Must run 100% offline with zero internet connectivity, 
  no cloud services (AWS, Azure), no external SaaS, and no externally hosted AI 
  APIs. All processing and models must be local.

[IN PLAIN WORDS: THE SCHOOL INSPECTOR ANALOGY]
Think of NCIIPC as government school inspectors and Critical Sector Entities 
(major banks, power grids, railways) as schools. When asked if they are 
teaching properly, every school submits a flawless binder: attendance is 100%, 
syllabus is marked completed, and compliance grades are an A+.

However, when inspectors open student notebooks at random, they find copied 
homework, uncorrected errors, and completely missing chapters.

In cybersecurity, entities report passing compliance audits. But when human 
inspectors read raw ticket files, they find analysts rubber-stamping critical 
alerts in 15 seconds to look fast, or discovering that core payment servers 
haven't emitted a single security log in six months.

Because human inspectors cannot read millions of records by hand, SAT-SA acts 
as an offline, automated auditing assistant that points inspectors directly to 
the entities and tickets cutting corners or operating in blind spots.

2. SOLUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 SUPERVISORY ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
Our proposed solution—the Supervisory Analytics Tool for SOC Assessment 
(SAT-SA)—is an offline, containerized analytical workbench. It ingests 
batch-submitted alert metadata, ticket records, and asset lists, processes them 
across modular detection engines, and presents inspectors with an explainable 
triage interface.

Rather than producing a single black-box risk score, the platform scores each 
entity along two independent axes that match the supervisory mandate directly:
1. Execution Integrity Score (EIS): Evaluates whether security teams are 
   genuinely investigating threats or merely closing tickets to hit operational 
   targets.
2. Coverage Assurance Score (CAS): Evaluates whether the entity's monitoring 
   radar is functional, or if critical systems and attack types are silently 
   missing.

[ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW IN PLAIN WORDS]
Instead of giving each bank a single, confusing score like "75% safe" (which 
hides what is actually wrong), we give them two completely separate report cards:
- Execution Integrity Score: Are their security guards actually doing honest 
  work, or are they just pretending to work by closing tickets without looking 
  at them?
- Coverage Assurance Score: Are all their security cameras turned on, or are 
  there massive blind spots where hackers can walk in unseen?
This dual system immediately tells the human inspector whether a bank has lazy 
workers, broken monitoring tools, or both.


2.2 DETECTING EXECUTION GAPS (EXECUTION INTEGRITY)
Execution gaps are detected through transparent, rule-driven, and semantic 
checks:
- SLA-Cliff Clustering Detection: Security operations frequently reward 
  resolving tickets within target time windows (e.g., under 30 minutes). When 
  analysts cut corners to meet metrics, alert closures cluster unnaturally right 
  before the deadline (e.g., minute 28 or 29) accompanied by minimal 
  investigation notes. The tool identifies these statistical clusters as 
  metric-gaming behavior.
- Escalation Logic Auditing: Critical-severity alerts occurring on Tier-1 core 
  assets (such as main database engines or industrial controllers) that are 
  closed without an accompanying escalation or incident record are flagged as 
  high-priority protocol breaches.
- Semantic Copy-Paste Analysis: Triage staff often paste standard boilerplates 
  (such as "Verified benign, user confirmed") across different alerts. An 
  embedded local language model compares investigation text across cases. 
  Near-identical text applied across unrelated hosts within short intervals is 
  flagged as superficial investigation.
- High-Velocity Rubber Stamping: Flags alerts involving complex multi-stage 
  attack techniques that were marked "resolved" within seconds of opening.

[DETECTING EXECUTION GAPS IN PLAIN WORDS]
This is our "cheat detector." It spots analysts who are cutting corners just to 
make their daily performance numbers look good:
- Waiting for the Timer: If tickets must be closed in 30 minutes, and an analyst 
  sits on them and closes 50 tickets at minute 29 with zero notes, our tool 
  catches them gaming the clock.
- Ignored Emergencies: If a red alert hits the main bank vault server and the 
  analyst clicks "Resolved" without ever notifying a manager, the tool sounds 
  an alarm.
- Copy-Paste Radar: If an analyst handles 100 alerts a day and pastes the exact 
  same excuse ("Checked with user, harmless") into 90 of them, our tool flags 
  them for fake investigations.


2.3 DETECTING NEGATIVE SPACE (COVERAGE ASSURANCE)
Negative space analysis assesses whether the lack of security evidence indicates 
operational health or unmonitored blind spots:
- Silent Asset Discovery: The tool compares an organization's master inventory 
  of critical servers against the list of servers that generated alerts. Any 
  high-criticality host that registers zero security events over an extended 
  period is flagged as an unmonitored blind spot.
- Expected Attack Category Mapping: Security infrastructure should register 
  common cyber activities. For instance, internet-facing assets are expected to 
  register external reconnaissance or access attempts. If an entity reports 
  thousands of generic notifications but zero alerts matching expected threat 
  classes, the system flags a detection blind spot.
- Peer Cohort Benchmarking: The tool groups entities by industry sector and scale 
  (e.g., large commercial banks). If nine banks average several hundred phishing 
  investigation records per month, but a tenth bank reports only one, the tool 
  flags the anomaly. The low count indicates a broken email ingestion sensor 
  rather than superior defenses.
- Telemetry Drop-Offs: The engine tracks long-term alert trends per entity. A 
  sudden, unexplained collapse in alert volume on a specific asset category is 
  flagged as a potential logging failure.

[DETECTING NEGATIVE SPACE IN PLAIN WORDS]
In cybersecurity, complete silence is dangerous. When a bank proudly claims, 
"We had zero alerts on our core database this month!", novice auditors think 
that is good news. Experienced inspectors know it means the alarms are unplugged.
- Silent Servers: If a company owns 500 critical servers, but 30 of them never 
  appear anywhere in the alert logs, our tool warns the inspector: "These 30 
  servers are completely unmonitored!"
- Peer Comparison: If 9 power companies report 200 blocked virus attempts a 
  week, but the 10th power company reports zero, their antivirus isn't better—
  their virus detection tool is simply broken.


2.4 ADVANCED FORENSIC DETECTION TECHNIQUES
Beyond the core execution-gap and negative-space checks described above, SAT-SA 
layers in a deeper set of forensic techniques aimed at coordinated, deliberate, 
and higher-order patterns of concealment—behaviour that no single rule or single 
statistical test would catch in isolation.

Extended Execution-Gap Detection:
- Throughput Implausibility Scoring (SAT-E6): For every analyst and shift, the 
  engine computes a maximum human-plausible investigation rate calibrated 
  against alert complexity and severity. Analysts or teams whose credited 
  closure volume statistically exceeds this ceiling—for example, over 150 "fully 
  investigated" high-severity tickets in a single eight-hour shift—are flagged 
  for review, independent of individual ticket content.
- Evidentiary Density Scoring (SAT-E7): A lightweight named-entity extraction 
  pass scans every investigation note for concrete technical artifacts: IP 
  addresses, file hashes, hostnames, account identifiers, vulnerability 
  references. Notes attached to high-severity alerts that contain zero specific 
  artifacts are scored as evidentiarily hollow, regardless of length or 
  grammatical polish—catching investigations that read convincingly but reference 
  nothing concrete.
- Escalation Theatre Detection (SAT-E8): Flags cases where an escalation record 
  is opened and then closed or reversed by the same actor within an implausibly 
  short window (under two minutes), a pattern consistent with a performative 
  escalation created to satisfy a compliance metric rather than to trigger 
  genuine incident response.
- Bulk-Closure Burst Detection (SAT-E9): Applies a sliding-window burst detector 
  across each entity's closure timestamps. A statistically improbable spike—
  dozens of unrelated alerts across different assets and categories, all closed 
  within the same sixty-second window—is flagged as a mass rubber-stamping event 
  rather than genuine individual triage.
- Severity Downgrade Auditing (SAT-E10): Cross-references the detection engine's 
  originally assigned severity against the analyst's final disposition severity. 
  Entities or analysts with an anomalously high rate of downgrading 
  high-confidence, rarely-benign technique classes immediately before closure 
  are flagged, since quietly relabelling a serious alert as harmless is a 
  well-known way to deflate an entity's apparent incident count.
- Hot-Potato Reassignment Analysis (SAT-E11): Builds a reassignment graph per 
  alert. Alerts passed between several analysts in the minutes before closure, 
  particularly just before a shift change, are flagged as likely ownership 
  avoidance rather than genuine collaborative investigation.
- Audit-Calendar Correlation (SAT-E12): Time-aligns each entity's operational 
  metrics against NCIIPC's known assessment and reporting calendar. A 
  statistically significant improvement in closure times or investigation depth 
  in the days immediately before a scheduled review, followed by reversion 
  afterward, is flagged as an "audit theatre" pattern.

Extended Negative-Space Detection:
- Remediation Decay-Curve Modelling (SAT-C5): For every recurring alert-asset 
  pair, the engine models the decay curve expected after a genuine fix. Pairs 
  that keep firing at a flat or increasing rate over successive periods, with no 
  decline consistent with remediation, indicate the underlying issue was 
  acknowledged repeatedly but never actually resolved.
- Asset Inventory Drift Detection (SAT-C6): Compares successive asset inventory 
  submissions. Assets that silently disappear without an accompanying 
  decommissioning record, and newly appearing assets with zero alert history in 
  their first reporting period, are both flagged—the former as possible scope 
  evasion, the latter as a monitoring lag.
- Red-Team Ground-Truth Reconciliation (SAT-C7): Where NCIIPC has visibility 
  into scheduled red-team or penetration-testing activity against a CSE, the tool 
  cross-references the known techniques and timing of that exercise against the 
  entity's alert data for the same window. A confirmed attack technique that 
  produced zero corresponding alerts becomes hard, ground-truth-validated 
  evidence of a detection gap, rather than a statistical inference.

Flagship Techniques (Meta-Level Forensics):
- Digit-Distribution Forensics (SAT-X1): Every numeric field in a submission—
  alert counts, closure durations, escalation counts—is checked against the 
  statistical digit-distribution patterns expected of naturally occurring 
  operational data, the same family of technique long used in financial forensic 
  auditing to catch fabricated ledgers. A submission whose numbers deviate 
  significantly from the expected distribution, or show suspicious clustering of 
  round numbers, is flagged not for a SOC process failure but for a possible 
  data-integrity problem in the submission itself—catching a CSV that was 
  manually smoothed or fabricated before it ever reached NCIIPC.
- Cross-Entity Sector Dark-Spot Meta-Analysis (SAT-X2): Individual coverage gaps 
  are aggregated across every CSE within a sector. If a specific technique shows 
  zero detections across nearly an entire sector rather than one entity, the tool 
  elevates the finding from an entity-level flag to a sector-level systemic blind 
  spot for NCIIPC's portfolio-level attention. This is the one finding class 
  SAT-SA produces that no single-entity audit, manual or automated, could ever 
  surface on its own.

[ADVANCED DETECTION TECHNIQUES IN PLAIN WORDS]
Beyond catching individual cheaters, this layer catches the smarter, more 
coordinated tricks:
- The Speed-Demon Catcher: If one analyst claims to have carefully checked 150 
  emergencies in a single shift, or 50 alarms all get closed in the same minute, 
  that's not diligence—that's a rubber stamp.
- The "Did You Even Look?" Test: If an investigation note never mentions a 
  single real detail—an IP address, a filename, anything specific—it's almost 
  certainly copied filler, however official it sounds.
- Fake Alarm-Pulling: Catches staff who technically raise an emergency and then 
  quietly cancel it themselves seconds later, or who keep passing a ticket around 
  so nobody has to actually own it.
- The Quiet Downgrade: Watches for alerts secretly relabelled from "dangerous" 
  to "harmless" right before closure, and for suspiciously good behaviour that 
  only ever shows up right before an inspection.
- Groundhog Day Check: If the same alarm keeps firing on the same server month 
  after month with nothing ever fixed, or a server quietly vanishes from the 
  inventory with no paperwork, both get flagged.
- The Planted Test: When NCIIPC secretly tests a bank's defences and the bank's 
  own system shows nothing happened, that's undeniable proof of a blind spot.
- The Fake-Numbers Detector: The same trick forensic accountants use to catch 
  cooked company books, repurposed to catch an entity that has literally smoothed 
  over or fabricated the security numbers it submitted.
- The Big-Picture View: If nearly every bank in the country has zero detections 
  for the exact same attack type, that isn't ten small problems—it's one 
  nationwide blind spot NCIIPC needs to fix industry-wide.


2.5 AIR-GAPPED, ZERO-COST TECHNOLOGY STACK
The entire application stack is built using open-source, locally executable 
components that require no internet connectivity or cloud subscriptions:

- User Interface: React, TypeScript, Recharts
  Lightweight, responsive browser dashboard served strictly over local network 
  loopback. Can also be packaged as a standalone offline desktop application.
- Application Engine: Python 3.11 with FastAPI
  Fast, deterministic API managing file ingestion, scoring rules, and 
  supervisory workflows.
- Analytics Store: DuckDB
  Embedded columnar database. Runs inside application memory to query millions 
  of flat records in seconds without database server overhead.
- Local NLP / ML: Sentence-Transformers (all-MiniLM)
  Lightweight open-weight semantic vector model running on CPU. Spots 
  copy-pasted notes without remote APIs.
- Narrative Synthesis: Quantized Local LLM (Llama-3.2 / Ollama)
  Fully local language model used strictly for drafting human-readable summary 
  memos for inspectors.
- Packaging: Docker Compose or Single Executable (.exe)
  Single-command container deployment or one-click Windows desktop package for 
  offline field laptops.

[TECHNOLOGY STACK IN PLAIN WORDS]
Because this tool handles national security data, it can never touch the 
internet. We cannot use cloud services like AWS or external APIs like OpenAI.
- Browser Dashboard or One-Click .EXE: Inspectors can open it inside Google 
  Chrome locally, or the developer can pitch it as a single, self-contained 
  desktop program (.exe) that an inspector runs with a double-click on any 
  offline laptop.
- DuckDB (Supercharged Excel): A lightning-fast database file built directly 
  into the app that can search through millions of rows of data in under a second.
- Local Mini-AI: A tiny, free AI model that lives on the inspector's computer. 
  It reads investigation notes and drafts summary reports without ever sending a 
  single word over the internet.
- Docker / Offline Installer: Packages our entire application into a single 
  folder so government staff can install it using an offline USB drive with one 
  click.


2.6 EXPLAINABILITY AND CRYPTOGRAPHIC AUDIT LEDGER
Supervisory actions demand strict evidence integrity and transparent 
justification:
- Deterministic Traceability: The tool does not use black-box decision models. 
  Every flagged entity allows the supervisor to drill down directly to the exact 
  underlying record IDs, timestamps, and specific heuristic rules triggered.
- Tamper-Evident Hash-Chain Ledger: To ensure findings stand up to regulatory 
  disputes, every ingested batch file receives an immediate cryptographic 
  fingerprint upon arrival. Every analytical step and supervisor finding is 
  sequentially linked into a tamper-evident audit ledger. If an audited entity 
  claims their submitted data was altered, the cryptographic chain verifies that 
  the findings match the original submission.

[EXPLAINABILITY AND AUDIT LEDGER IN PLAIN WORDS]
If NCIIPC imposes a fine on a bank for cutting corners, the bank's lawyers might 
say: "Your tool made a mistake, or someone edited our spreadsheet!"
- No Black Boxes: Our tool doesn't just say "Bank X is bad." It provides the 
  exact ticket numbers, analyst names, and timestamps that caused the flag.
- Digital Wax Seal (The Ledger): The instant NCIIPC receives a bank's data files, 
  the tool takes a permanent cryptographic fingerprint of the files. Every 
  finding is chained to that fingerprint. If anyone alters even a single comma 
  in the records later, the chain breaks. This guarantees 100% proof in 
  regulatory reviews or legal proceedings.

2.8 FINAL SOLUTION SUMMARY: THE END-TO-END WORKFLOW EXPLAINED

Step 1: Data Preparation and Physical Handover
- It is NOT Raw System Logs or Packet Captures: We do not touch terabytes of raw 
  computer logs, firewall dumps, customer passwords, or network packet 
  recordings. Those are too massive, contain private user information, and are 
  out of scope.
- It IS Alert Metadata and Case-Management Spreadsheets: What the audited 
  entities (banks, power companies, etc.) actually give NCIIPC is structured 
  operational summary files—typically exported in standard CSV, JSON, or Excel 
  database dumps. Think of it like a cybersecurity ticketing sheet. It has 
  organized rows and columns containing:
    * Ticket/Alert ID (e.g., ALT-90214)
    * Timestamp Created and Timestamp Closed
    * Threat Category (e.g., Ransomware, Brute-Force, Phishing)
    * Assigned Analyst Name or Badge ID
    * Target Asset Identity (e.g., Core-Database-Server-01)
    * Escalation Status (Yes/No)
    * Analyst Investigation Notes (the text the human analyst typed)
    * Asset Inventory Master List (a separate list of all servers the company owns)
- How it arrives: Because these critical infrastructure organizations and NCIIPC 
  are disconnected from the public internet, the audited entity exports these CSV 
  files at the end of each audit cycle, copies them onto an encrypted, physical 
  government-approved USB storage drive, and hand-delivers them to NCIIPC 
  headquarters.

Step 2: Secure Application Launch on Air-Gapped Workstations
- Local Web Browser Mode: The tool runs inside Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge, 
  but it is strictly local (http://127.0.0.1:8000). It requires zero internet 
  access; the browser is just acting as a clean, responsive display screen.
- The One-Click Desktop Executable (.exe): To make it as easy as possible for 
  examiners on offline field laptops, the developer can bundle the entire 
  frontend, Python backend, embedded database, and local AI into a single 
  standalone installer or .exe file (using packaging tools like PyInstaller with 
  Electron/Tauri). The inspector does not need to install Python, configure 
  servers, or touch a terminal. They simply double-click the icon, and the 
  software launches instantly on their offline workstation.
- Built-In Hardening on Launch: The tool binds exclusively to localhost 
  (127.0.0.1), completely ignoring any external network interfaces. This 
  prevents any other device on the local office network from connecting to or 
  snooping on the inspector's active session.

Step 3: Ingestion, Validation, and the Cryptographic Wax Seal
- Strict Input Sanitization and File Validation: When CSV or JSON files are 
  uploaded, the backend does not execute or evaluate raw code. It strictly 
  validates file structures and column headers against rigid schemas. This 
  prevents CSV injection attacks, SQL injections, and malicious buffer overflows.
- Instant Cryptographic Fingerprinting: The very millisecond the file is loaded, 
  the software calculates a cryptographic hash (using the military-grade SHA-256 
  algorithm). A hash is a unique 64-character fingerprint of the file. If a file 
  has 500,000 lines of data, the algorithm creates one exact fingerprint string.
- How the Ledger Protects NCIIPC and the Inspector: If an audited bank later 
  faces a massive government fine for neglecting security, their legal team might 
  claim: "The NCIIPC inspector accidentally edited our numbers, or your tool 
  modified our files!" 
  This is where the audit ledger comes in. The software logs the raw file's hash, 
  the exact software version, the date and time, and every generated finding into 
  an immutable, tamper-evident hash-chain (similar to a local blockchain ledger). 
  If anyone changes even a single letter, number, or space in that CSV file, the 
  fingerprint will never match again. This gives NCIIPC 100% legal and 
  regulatory proof that the findings are genuine and derived directly from what 
  the entity submitted.

Step 4: Automated Analytical Interrogation (Detection Checks)
A. Catching Execution Gaps (Faked Work & Metric-Gaming):
  * SLA-Cliff Watcher (SAT-E1): If analysts have a target to resolve tickets 
    within 30 minutes, and the tool notices a massive spike of tickets marked 
    closed at minutes 28 or 29 with almost no notes written, it catches them 
    sitting on tickets and closing them just before a penalty clock hits.
  * The Copy-Paste Radar (SAT-E2): An offline AI reads analyst notes across 
    different tickets. If an analyst resolved 100 tickets today and used the 
    exact same text ("User verified, false positive") on 80 of them, the tool 
    flags it as fake, mechanical triage.
  * Flash Ticket Closer / Rubber-Stamping (SAT-E3): Complex hacker alerts take 
    time to analyze. If a critical malware alert is opened and closed in 15 
    seconds flat, the tool flags that no human could have possibly investigated 
    it in that time.
  * Ignored Emergencies (SAT-E4): If an alert involves a core payment server or 
    nuclear control asset and is labeled "Critical Severity," but the analyst 
    closed it without forwarding it to an emergency response team, it is 
    immediately flagged as a high-risk violation.
  * Superficial Triage Pattern (SAT-E5): Flags accounts that repeatedly mark 
    alerts resolved without checking connected systems, running IP lookups, or 
    taking any containment action.
  * The Speed-Demon Catcher (SAT-E6): Calculates the maximum humanly possible 
    tickets an analyst can investigate in an 8-hour shift. If someone claims to 
    have thoroughly reviewed 200 high-severity alarms in one day, the tool flags 
    that humanly impossible workload.
  * The Empty-Note Detector (SAT-E7): A real technical investigation always 
    mentions concrete details like an IP address, a filename, or a username. If 
    an analyst writes long, professional-sounding essays that contain zero real 
    technical facts or indicators, it flags the note as empty filler.
  * Fake Alarm-Pulling (SAT-E8): Catches staff who escalate a ticket to look 
    compliant on paper, but quietly cancel and close the escalation 60 seconds 
    later before anyone actually investigates.
  * The Bulk-Closure Stamp (SAT-E9): If 60 unrelated alarms across 30 different 
    servers are all marked "Closed" within the exact same 10-second window, the 
    tool flags a mass rubber-stamping event.
  * The Quiet Downgrade (SAT-E10): If an analyst frequently changes "Critical" 
    or "High" danger alarms into "Low" or "Informational" right before closing 
    them, the tool flags them for secretly deflating their incident count to 
    look good on reports.
  * Hot-Potato Passing (SAT-E11): Detects tickets that were rapidly reassigned 
    between 4 different analysts right before a shift change so that nobody had 
    to take responsibility for solving it.
  * Audit-Theatre Detector (SAT-E12): Compares performance against the NCIIPC 
    calendar. If an organization's response speed and quality magically improve 
    400% the week before an inspection and collapse back to lazy habits the week 
    after, the tool flags them for staging an audit show.

B. Catching Negative Space (Blind Spots & Broken Cameras):
  * Silent Server Finder (SAT-C1): Compares the company's server inventory 
    against alert files. If 25 high-value servers never appear in any security 
    logs all quarter, the tool alerts inspectors that these machines are 
    unmonitored blind spots.
  * Missing Attack Categories (SAT-C2): Cross-references expected hacker attacks. 
    If an entity runs public web servers but reports zero external scans or web 
    attacks over 90 days, the tool reveals that their web-monitoring rules are 
    broken.
  * Peer Benchmarking Radar (SAT-C3): Compares similar companies. If 9 power 
    companies report an average of 300 blocked phishing attempts a month, but a 
    10th power company reports only 2, the tool flags them for having a broken 
    email filter.
  * Sudden Log Drop-Offs (SAT-C4): Detects when a server that normally sends 50 
    alerts a week suddenly drops to zero alerts with no explanation, catching 
    broken logging pipelines.
  * Groundhog Day / Fake Fix Check (SAT-C5): When an issue is genuinely fixed, 
    its alerts disappear. If the exact same alert keeps firing on the exact same 
    server week after week, the tool flags that the security team is closing 
    tickets without ever fixing the root problem.
  * Vanishing Inventory Drift (SAT-C6): Catches servers that quietly disappear 
    from inventory lists without decommission paperwork, or new servers that are 
    connected with zero security monitoring active.
  * The Planted Test Reconciliation (SAT-C7): If NCIIPC conducts an authorized 
    simulated attack exercise, the tool cross-checks the exact time of the 
    exercise against the entity's CSV submission. If the simulated attack 
    produced zero alerts, it provides indisputable proof of an operational blind 
    spot.

C. Meta-Level Forensics (Catching Fabricated Data & Systemic Issues):
  * The Fake-Numbers Detector (SAT-X1): Naturally occurring numbers in real life 
    follow predictable statistical distributions (the same law forensic 
    accountants use to catch cooked financial books). If an organization 
    manually makes up or smooths their numbers in Excel before giving the CSV 
    to NCIIPC, this test detects the unnatural number patterns and flags the 
    submission as fabricated.
  * Nationwide Dark-Spot Meta-Analysis (SAT-X2): Aggregates blind spots across 
    all entities. If nearly every bank in the country is failing to detect the 
    exact same ransomware technique, the tool alerts NCIIPC leadership to an 
    industry-wide national security gap.

Step 5: Supervisory Dashboard Review and Deep-Dive Auditing
- Priority Leaderboard: Instead of reading 100,000 spreadsheet rows by hand, 
  the dashboard lists entities sorted by risk: "Out of 50 audited CSEs, begin 
  review with CSE-005 and CSE-003."
- Dual Scorecards (EIS and CAS): The inspector instantly sees whether an entity 
  is cutting corners (low EIS) or missing monitoring coverage (low CAS).
- One-Click Drill-Down Evidence: Every flagged issue points directly to the 
  underlying raw records. Clicking on an alert flag displays the exact alert 
  IDs, timestamps, analyst names, and notes that triggered the finding. No 
  decisions rely on an unexplained black box.
- Automated Narrative Drafting: The local quantized language model drafts a 
  concise, factual summary memo of the findings for the inspector to attach to 
  their official assessment dossier.

Step 6: Session Teardown, Cryptographic Archival, and Memory Purge
- Encrypted Local Archival: The generated inspection report and the hash-chain 
  ledger entries are written to local disk encrypted with AES-256. Even if the 
  laptop is physically stolen, unauthorized parties cannot decrypt the findings 
  without the master key.
- Zero-Data Retention and Memory Wiping: The software executes an active memory 
  purge: all cached notes, in-memory DuckDB tables, and temporary vector 
  embeddings are completely flushed from the RAM.
- Zero Model Feedback: The local AI model does not train or adjust its weights 
  on the entity's data. It retains nothing in memory. If another inspector opens 
  the software next, no residual notes or evidence from the previous assessment 
  can be viewed or recovered.
- Model Integrity Check on Shutdown and Boot: The cryptographic checksum of the 
  AI model weights is confirmed before final shutdown, ensuring the offline 
  binary remains completely uncorrupted for future audits.
